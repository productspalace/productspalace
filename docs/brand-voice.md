# Products Palace brand voice

How Products Palace sounds, and the rules for writing anything the website says.
Source of truth: **Products Palace Brand Story** (Lagos, 2026). When this guide and the brand story
disagree, the brand story wins. Reused copy lives in `src/content/brand.ts`, and homepage copy
lives in `src/content/home.ts`.

## 1. Purpose

Products Palace exists for one moment. The gate closes behind you, the day lets go, and home gives
you back to yourself and to the people you love. The brand began with one question: *what does a
home owe the person who works this hard?* Its answer is peace, safety, unhurried time with family,
and a quiet place to think about the future.

## 2. Positioning

A Lagos developer of homes for ambitious professionals, couples and families, designed around a
**season of life** rather than a specification sheet. The homes are a means. The life they make
possible is the product.

- **Category:** residential developments in a protected, managed community.
- **For:** people who carry Lagos's ambition and want a home that gives something back.
- **Unlike most luxury property brands:** luxury is never about status. It is the foundation that
  makes a good life possible.

## 3. Philosophy

| Idea | What we mean | What we never mean |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Luxury** | Whatever makes a good life possible: security, stillness, time, privacy. *"Not the destination, but the firm ground you stand on while you build what truly matters."* | One person above another. More marble. A chandelier to photograph. |
| **Exclusivity** | A protected community, so parents choose the influences around their children and home is a place of rest. *"Exclusive, for a reason."* | Feeling superior to the world outside the gate. |
| **Home** | A place that supports ambition, relationships, rest and safety. | An asset, a trophy or a stage for performance. |

Tagline, used verbatim and never paraphrased: **Luxury, for a purpose.**

## 4. Personality

Products Palace speaks like a thoughtful friend who has lived the same Lagos week as you, and who
happens to build homes.

| We are | Without being |
| --- | --- |
| Warm and human | Familiar or chatty |
| Refined | Pretentious |
| Calm and reassuring | Passive or vague |
| Confident | Boastful |
| Aspirational | Out of reach |
| Intimate | Intrusive |
| Poetic | Unclear |

## 5. Tone of voice

- **Second person, present tense, specific moments.** "It is Friday night and neither of you wants
  to go out." Scenes beat adjectives.
- **Local, lightly.** Owambe, jollof, the generator next door, boys' quarters, the traffic. One
  detail per scene is enough.
- **Short sentences, then a longer one that breathes.** Read it aloud: it should sound calm.
- **British English** (neighbour, colour, programme, towards). Curly apostrophes and quotes (’ “ ”)
  in content files.
- **Title Case** for section H2s, which matches the brand story. **Sentence case** for everything
  else: hero headline, card titles, buttons, navigation.
- **No exclamation marks.** No ALL-CAPS in source copy; the design uppercases where it needs to.

## 7. Audiences: four seasons of life

| Household | Season of life | The feeling to give them | Home (brand story) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **The one who is building** | Focused professional | Thinking clearly; rest; ambition with room to breathe | Not tied to a size; homes sit close enough to the city to make every hour count |
| **The newlyweds** | A new marriage | Evenings that belong to the two of them; privacy; "the good old days" | Two-bedroom maisonette |
| **The growing family** | Young children | Reassurance; childhood memories; community that shares your values | Three-bedroom maisonette |
| **The family that has arrived** | Established, multi-generational | Gathering generations; the reward of years of work; dignity | Four-bedroom maisonette |

Speak to the season of life first and the bedroom count second.

## 8. Messaging principles

1. **Sell the life, not the building.** Lead with the moment, then support it with the fact.
2. **One feeling per section.** If two sections say the same thing, cut one.
3. **Show the scene; don't name the emotion.** "Nobody drops in unannounced" says more than
   "unparalleled privacy".
4. **Respect the reader's intelligence.** No hard sell and no urgency tactics.
5. **Planned means planned.** The pilot development is a plan. Its homes and amenities "will be"
   or are "planned". They are never described as built, open or available.
6. **Promises are commitments, not guarantees.** Write what we design for, and never claim an
   outcome we cannot verify.

## 9. Emotional copywriting framework

**Physical feature → practical benefit → emotional outcome → life experience.** Write the life
experience. Keep the feature in view so the claim stays honest.

| Feature | Benefit | Outcome | Life experience (the copy) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Gated, managed community | Controlled access | Peace of mind | "The gate opens, and the guard greets you by name." |
| Boys' quarters with every home | Live-in help close by | Support without losing privacy | "The help you rely on is close at hand and your privacy stays your own." |
| Padel court | Shared recreation | Friendship | "Weekends that turn neighbours into friends." |
| Children's play area | Safe space to play | Reassurance, joy | "You know every child in the play area and every parent watching from its edge." |
| Generous parking | Space for every car | Easy arrivals and gatherings | "Every car has found a space." |
| Location chosen for the commute | Shorter journeys | Time and less frustration | "Minutes won back from traffic and spent at the dinner table instead." |
| Four bedrooms | Room for family and guests | Togetherness with privacy | "Your parents are settled in the guest room." |

## 10. Vocabulary

**Prefer:** home, come home, season of life, room (to grow, to think, for everyone), quiet,
stillness, peace, privacy, safe, protected, neighbours, community, gather, unhurried, the people
you love, our first address, planned, picture it, purpose.

**Use with care:** *luxury* (only in the brand's redefined sense, or in the tagline);
*exclusive* (only with its reason); *maisonette*, *boys' quarters* (facts, in support of a feeling).

**Avoid:** experience unparalleled luxury · redefining modern living · where luxury meets comfort ·
world-class · unmatched sophistication · your dream home awaits · elevate your lifestyle · the
epitome of elegance · luxury like never before · prestigious address · state-of-the-art · nestled ·
bespoke · exquisite · iconic · premium living · investment opportunity · limited units · act now ·
best · No. 1.

## 11. Effective and ineffective copy

| Ineffective | Why it fails | Effective |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "Experience unparalleled luxury living." | Generic and status-led; says nothing | "A home for the life you’re working towards." |
| "Spacious 4-bedroom units with premium finishes." | Specification without a reason to care | "Room for everyone you love." |
| "24/7 world-class security." | An unverifiable guarantee | "A secure, managed community, so you stop checking the locks twice." |
| "State-of-the-art padel court." | A feature presented as finished | "A padel court for the Saturday game." (in a section marked *Planned*) |
| "Just 10 minutes from the island!" | An invented, unconfirmed claim | "Locations chosen with the commute in mind." |
| "Exclusive living for the elite." | Superiority | "Exclusive, for a reason." |

## 12. Calls to action

- **Say what happens next.** The label must describe the destination: *Register your interest*
  opens the registration form; *Who we build for* scrolls to the households; *See where they
  begin* scrolls to the first address.
- **Invite; don't push.** No "now", "today", "hurry" or "limited".
- **Two to five words, sentence case.** The design uppercases buttons.
- **One primary action site-wide: register interest.** Until there are homes to view, everything
  converges on it.
- Approved set: *Register your interest*, *Register interest* (tight spaces), *Who we build for*,
  *Find your place*, *See where they begin*, *Let’s talk about home* (contact heading).

## 13. Content accuracy rules

1. Only state property facts that are in the brand story or another approved document. Today
   that means two, three and four-bedroom maisonettes, boys' quarters with each, a padel court,
   a children's play area, generous parking and a protected, managed community, all **planned**.
2. Never invent prices, addresses, completion dates, timelines, testimonials, ratings, awards,
   years in business, sales numbers, returns, construction specifications, commute times,
   amenities or security certifications.
3. Unknown details stay neutral ("We’ll share the full details of the homes in time") or are
   clear placeholders marked `PLACEHOLDER` in the content files.
4. Imagery must not imply amenities that are not planned. In particular, there is no pool in
   the brand story.
5. Narrative scenes ("the guard greets you by name") describe the life the brand designs for.
   Keep them clearly in story form, not in fact lists.
